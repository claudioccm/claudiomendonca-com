The quote is still open, and now you’re wondering whether it’s too late to follow up without making things awkward. A quote follow-up email prompt can help you draft a courteous message while the job is fresh, then you can put the next action somewhere you’ll remember it.

The awkward part often grows while you’re busy doing the work.

You meant to check in, but the quote slipped down the inbox. Here’s a practical way to prepare a few messages now, decide when they make sense, and keep the follow-up from disappearing again.

## How Can You Follow Up on an Open Quote This Week?

To follow up on an open quote, write a brief, relevant message that makes it easy for the customer to respond. Drafting a few options when you send the quote can spare you the effort of rebuilding the context later.

Then set a reminder for the next action, based on the quote and any timing you agreed with the customer. Fieldmotion describes the familiar pattern: the longer a quote sits, the more awkward it feels to chase it, until an owner either stops following up altogether or fires off the same message until it starts to feel embarrassing.

Its [suggested sequence](https://fieldmotion.com/blog/how-to-follow-up-on-quotes/) is a courtesy check after 24–48 hours, a more useful follow-up after 3–5 days, a different channel or angle after 1–2 weeks, and then a closing message. Treat this as one practical example, not a universal schedule. Jobber suggests following up when a customer has not replied within 2–3 days, while ServiceTitan recommends starting within 48 hours.[^1]

The right timing depends on the work, the customer’s situation, and whether they gave you a date to check back. If you agreed to follow up next week, follow that agreement instead of sending an earlier nudge. A genuine quote expiry date can also be useful context, but don’t invent a deadline to force a decision.

Try this sequence on the day you send a quote:

1. Open the quote and note what the customer asked about, what work it covers, and any question still to resolve.
2. Ask an AI assistant to draft three short messages using those details.
3. Check every draft, correct the names and facts, and choose a reminder date for each message you may send.

Copy and adapt this quote follow-up email prompt:

> Draft three brief, courteous follow-up messages for this quote. Use a helpful, low-pressure tone and plain language.  
>  
> Customer: [CUSTOMER NAME]  
> Work quoted: [SHORT DESCRIPTION]  
> Quote sent: [DATE]  
> Customer’s question or concern: [DETAILS, OR “NONE KNOWN”]  
> Any agreed follow-up date or genuine expiry date: [DATE OR “NONE”]  
> Preferred contact method: [EMAIL, TEXT, OR OTHER]  
>  
> Message 1: A simple check-in that confirms the quote arrived and invites questions.  
> Message 2: A useful follow-up that addresses the customer’s question or offers a clear next step. Do not repeat the first message.  
> Message 3: A close-the-loop message that makes it easy for the customer to say no, pause the project, or ask to revisit it later.  
>  
> Do not invent job details, urgency, availability, or prices. Keep each message under 80 words. Give me subject lines for email. Do not send anything.

Read the drafts before sending. Take out anything that sounds unlike you, check the scope and dates against the actual quote, and use the customer’s preferred channel where you know it.

An AI draft is a starting point, not a reason to send a message that does not fit the conversation.

Silence does not always mean a customer has chosen another contractor. Fieldmotion suggests it may mean the customer is delayed or finds it uncomfortable to say no. Instead of asking for a decision immediately, try a question that gives them something simple to answer: “Where have you got to with it?” or “Is there anything on the quote that doesn’t make sense?”

The last message should make a clear reply easy. For example: “If the timing’s changed or you’ve decided not to go ahead, no problem. Just let me know and I’ll close this off for now.”

Fieldmotion recommends a final message that removes the pressure to keep the conversation open. A more direct question such as “Have you given up on this project?” is better saved for a late-stage message; the [School of SDR article](https://schoolofsdr.substack.com/p/have-you-given-up-on-this-project) warns that it can backfire if used too early or with the wrong person.

## When Does a Follow-Up System Help More Than Another Email?

A follow-up system helps when the main problem is remembering what to do next, not finding the right words. For each quote, keep its status, next follow-up date, last message or channel, and any customer response in one place — whether that is a shared list, an inbox flag, or a calendar reminder you actually check.

The tool matters less than the habit of looking.

Start with a simple version this week. Add four fields to your quote list: status, next follow-up date, last contact method, and customer response.

When the customer replies, update the status and remove or change any reminder that no longer applies. If you use a calendar, put the customer and quote reference in the reminder so you know what it means when it appears.

A draft in a chat window is not a scheduled follow-up.

The reminder has to live somewhere you will see it. ChatGPT’s [Scheduled Tasks documentation](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/10291617-scheduled-tasks-in-chatgpt) describes task reminders and notifications, but that is different from a quote workflow that knows whether a customer has replied. Whatever tool you use, decide who checks the list and what happens when the customer answers.

A sequence works better when each message has a different job. Fieldmotion’s schedule is one example of spacing out touches and changing the approach instead of sending the same vague nudge repeatedly.

If the customer asked a question, answer it. If the timing is uncertain, ask when they would like you to check back. If they decline or ask you not to contact them, update the record and stop the sequence.

HubSpot’s “Permission To Close Your File” template gives another option for a final message. HubSpot reports a 76% response rate for the template after two or three unanswered follow-ups.[^2]

That figure comes from the template report; it is not a promise that the same wording or response rate will apply to your business. The useful idea is the permission to close the conversation, not a magic sentence.

A shared workflow may help when several people need to know whether a quote is waiting on the customer, waiting on the business, or ready for scheduling. Jobber’s [help documentation](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/automations) describes quote reminders by email or text, with scheduling based on how many days have passed since the quote was sent and the customer’s communication settings.

ServiceTitan’s [estimate follow-up report](https://help.servicetitan.com/docs/opportunity-and-estimate-follow-up-report) documents fields such as estimate status and scheduled follow-up date. These are examples of what a system can track, not a recommendation that every small team needs either product.

Before adding software, look at the work you already do. If your quote list and calendar show who owns the next step, keep using them.

A CRM or field-service system becomes more useful when several people need to update the same quote, customer details are split between tools, or the owner is the only one who can answer “Where are we with this?” The system’s job is to keep the quote, status, next action, and response visible together.

## What Does This Look Like with an AI Assistant You Can Talk To?

An [AI assistant connected to the tools a business already uses](/writing/trade-business-job-scheduling-paper-diary-ai-assistant) can turn a spoken update into a record, a calendar change, and a draft customer message. The owner still decides what to send, but the follow-up does not have to be reconstructed from a notebook, inbox, and memory. This scene is an illustration of what today's AI tools can do, not a client story.

An electrician is between jobs. They speak to ChatGPT voice mode: “The kitchen quote is still open, the customer asked about the start date, and I’ve finished the site visit. Move the reminder to Thursday and let them know I’ll confirm availability.” The assistant updates the quote record in Jobber, moves the reminder in Google Calendar, and prepares a message that answers the customer’s question without promising a start date the electrician has not checked.

The electrician glances at the draft and approves it, and nothing gets typed twice: the customer receives a clear update, and the record shows what the business said and what still needs to be confirmed.

Later, the electrician asks, “Check which quotes are waiting on me and which are waiting on the customer.” The assistant brings the overdue follow-up to the top, identifies the customer’s latest response, and drafts a close-the-loop message.

The electrician reviews it and says yes. When the customer replies, the assistant updates the quote status so the next person opening the record can see the answer.

Before heading home, the electrician says, “Get tomorrow’s follow-ups ready and make sure the team can see what’s waiting.” The assistant organizes the next actions in the shared quote records, checks the calendar, and leaves each open quote with a clear status and next step. Xero is connected for the business’s accounting records, alongside Jobber and Google Calendar, so the work stays in the tools the team uses rather than in a separate list of loose notes.

A teammate can open the quote record and answer, “Where are we with this?” The electrician reads through the messages and confirms the customer details before anything goes out. The day ends with the follow-ups in view and the job notes attached to the quote, not scattered across a notebook, inbox, and memory.

## Conclusion

Write the messages while the quote details are fresh, make it easy for the customer to ask a question or close the conversation, and put the next action somewhere you will see it. A short prompt can help with the wording; a clear reminder is what brings the task back when you’re busy.

Choose one open quote today. Draft its next message, check it against the quote, and set a reminder to send it.
