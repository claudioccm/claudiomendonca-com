There are two kinds of AI work inside a company. The repeatable kind you can hand to a system, and the messy middle — the one-offs, the judgment calls, the "I just need this once, well" tasks. The first kind you automate. The second kind you train people to do better.

## Why training, not tooling

You can't build a pipeline for every ad-hoc task. There are too many, they're too varied, and half of them are unique. What you can do is raise the floor on how well your team uses the tools they already have for that long tail. That's training, and it's the part most companies skip because it doesn't come with a dashboard.

## What a good session looks like

It isn't a slide deck about prompting. It's hands on the keyboard, with real tasks from the actual job:

- Bring the thing you did badly last week. We'll do it again, properly.
- Watch where the model gives up, and learn what to feed it instead.
- Build a small library of patterns the team will reuse — not generic tips.

## The outcome you're after

The goal isn't that everyone becomes a prompt engineer. It's that the team develops judgment — a feel for which tasks are worth handing to a model, which need a human, and how to tell the difference fast. Tools change every few months. That judgment compounds for years.

A good session leaves people faster on the messy middle and clearer about where AI helps and where it just makes confident mistakes. That clarity is the real deliverable.
